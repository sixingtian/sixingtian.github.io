## About

I am **Xingtian (James) Si**, an M.A. student in Economics at the **University of Chicago** (2026–2027). I received my B.Sc. in Economics from the **University of Nottingham Ningbo China** (dual degree with the University of Nottingham, UK), graduating with **First-Class Honours**, a GPA of **3.90/4.0**, and a **top-5%** rank.

I am a researcher in **causal inference and causal machine learning**, with a focus on **consumer behavior, targeting, and policy evaluation**.

My interests center on estimating **heterogeneous treatment effects** and designing decision rules for **who to treat / what to recommend**, using modern tools such as **Double/Debiased Machine Learning (DML)**, **TMLE**, **causal forests**, and related methods. I am especially interested in bridging econometrics and machine learning to build **interpretable, policy-relevant** evidence and scalable targeting systems.

## Research Interests

- Causal inference & program evaluation (selection, identification, robustness)
- Causal ML (DML / TMLE / causal forests; HTE / uplift modeling)
- Targeting and personalization in marketing (treatment assignment, incremental impact)
- Applied microeconomics and data-driven policy design

## Current Projects

- **Correspondence audit studies on labor market discrimination**: research assistant for Prof. Sally Sadoff (Rady School of Management, UC San Diego) — reviewing 22 correspondence audit studies, auditing a 53-sheet database of 600+ fictitious names, and harmonizing eight name-perception studies into a dataset of 1,600+ names and 25 variables.
- **Plug-in data thinning in econometric panels**: independent research on the validity boundaries of plug-in data thinning — when consistent covariance estimation fails to deliver valid choose-then-infer procedures, with Monte Carlo evidence from DiD, event-study pretesting, and covariate screening.
- **Subscription regulation theory**: co-authored research (supervised by Assoc. Prof. Haibo Xu) on how capping cancellation friction (e.g., California's AB 2863) can raise firms' return to retention-menu complexity through a substitution mechanism.

**News**: my paper *Causal Effects of Coupon Expiration on Consumer Acceptance* was published in **Applied Economics** (Oct 2026). [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/00036846.2026.2750980)
