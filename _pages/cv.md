## Research Experience

**Correspondence Audit Studies on Labor Market Discrimination**, San Diego, CA (May 2026 – Present)  
Research Assistant to Prof. Sally Sadoff, Rady School of Management, UC San Diego  
- Systematically reviewed 22 correspondence audit studies to document how fictitious names signal race and gender in labor-market discrimination research; constructed and audited a 53-sheet database of 600+ names, including name pairings and source metadata  
- Harmonized eight name-perception studies into a dataset of 1,600+ names and 25 variables, including race and gender, perception accuracy and birth-record frequencies; prepared a data dictionary and cross-study overlap analysis  
- Processed data from 10,000+ respondents and 44,000+ observations in Python to construct race and gender perception accuracy measures and an analysis-ready participant-level dataset

**When Does Plug-in Data Thinning Work?**, Chicago, IL (Aug 2026 – Present)  
Independent Research  
- Derived an asymptotic characterization of the dependence induced by plug-in covariance estimation in clustered panels, demonstrating that consistent covariance estimation does not guarantee joint independence of thinned data components  
- Established sufficient conditions for asymptotically valid post-selection inference based on fixed-dimensional linear aggregates, and characterized how aggregation, covariance misspecification, and error-direction alignment affect inferential validity  
- Conducted Monte Carlo experiments involving difference-in-differences, event-study pretesting, and covariate screening, alongside a county-level minimum-wage placebo application, to evaluate conditional rejection rates and compare plug-in thinning with cluster splitting, exact rotation, and conditional inference

**When Click-to-Cancel Creates Complexity: A Theory of Subscription Regulation**, Ningbo, China & Chicago, IL (Apr 2026 – Present)  
Co-authored Research, supervised by Assoc. Prof. Haibo Xu, University of Nottingham Ningbo China  
- Developed a theoretical model in which firms jointly choose cancellation friction and retention-menu complexity; derived a substitution mechanism whereby tighter cancellation restrictions expose more consumers to retention offers, increasing the return to complexity  
- Derived conditions under which induced complexity offsets the consumer benefits of easier cancellation, incorporating time costs borne by consumers who ultimately cancel; constructed a solved example showing that the consumer-welfare-maximizing friction cap can be interior  
- Extended the model to examine friction-induced fatigue and joint regulation of both instruments; characterized when the complexity response can reverse and how joint constraints eliminate endogenous complexity adjustment

**Causal Machine Learning and Counterfactual Inference in Consumer Behavior**, Ningbo, China (Sep 2025 – May 2026)  
Dissertation, supervised by Assoc. Prof. Zhenjiang Lin, University of Nottingham Ningbo China  
- Studied two-hour versus one-day coupon windows using 12,007 scenario-survey responses across 490 demographic profiles; under selection on observables, cross-fitted IPW estimates an 18.3 percentage point reduction in stated acceptance (cluster-robust SE: 1.4 pp)  
- Separated heterogeneity discovery from confirmation through profile-cluster sample splitting, using causal forests and T-learners to explore effect variation and a held-out sample to evaluate contrasts by urgency, social context and distance  
- Compared effect-based targeting with propensity-based and random allocation under a fixed short-expiration coupon budget; simulations showed 29–42% lower acceptance losses than random allocation, with uncertainty assessed using 500-replication cluster-bootstrap intervals

**Early Childhood Intervention and Mathematics Achievement**, New York, NY (Mar 2025 – Sep 2025)  
Research Assistant to Dr. Jiaxi Yang (Ph.D., Columbia University)  
- Analyzed baseline differences between special-education and general-education cohorts across six demographic, academic, and family-related domains, using standardized mean differences and variance ratios to assess group comparability and potential selection bias  
- Applied causal random forests to investigate heterogeneous effects of special-education services on fifth-grade mathematics achievement across students with different socioeconomic and academic backgrounds  
- Developed interactive dashboards in R Shiny and Python Dash to visualize subgroup-level treatment-effect estimates

**Do Female Directors Improve the Financial Performance of Companies**, Nottingham, UK (Nov 2024 – Jun 2025)  
Team Member, supervised by Prof. Abigail Barr, University of Nottingham  
- Conducted econometric analysis of 840 UK firm-year observations (2008–2016) using Stata to examine the relationship between board gender diversity and corporate financial performance (ROA and Tobin's Q)  
- Compared linear and nonlinear specifications, finding an inverted-U relationship between female board representation and Tobin's Q, with the estimated turning point around 20–25%
