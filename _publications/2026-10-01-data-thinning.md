This paper derives an asymptotic characterization of the dependence induced by plug-in covariance estimation in clustered panels, demonstrating that consistent covariance estimation does not guarantee joint independence of thinned data components. It establishes sufficient conditions for asymptotically valid post-selection inference based on fixed-dimensional linear aggregates, and characterizes how aggregation, covariance misspecification, and error-direction alignment affect inferential validity. Monte Carlo experiments — covering difference-in-differences, event-study pretesting, and covariate screening — and a county-level minimum-wage placebo application compare plug-in thinning with cluster splitting, exact rotation, and conditional inference.
