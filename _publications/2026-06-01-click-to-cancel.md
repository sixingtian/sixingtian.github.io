We develop a partial-equilibrium model in which firms jointly choose cancellation friction and retention-menu complexity, and prove a "waterbed effect" whereby capping friction under California's AB 2863 raises firms' return to complexity. We derive the welfare condition under which click-to-cancel regulation benefits consumers, showing that only comprehensive regulation constraining both instruments eliminates the waterbed effect.
