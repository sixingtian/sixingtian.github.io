We develop a theoretical model in which firms jointly choose cancellation friction and retention-menu complexity, and derive a substitution mechanism: tighter cancellation restrictions (e.g., click-to-cancel rules such as California's AB 2863) expose more consumers to retention offers, increasing the return to complexity in the baseline model. We derive conditions under which induced complexity offsets the consumer benefits of easier cancellation — incorporating time costs borne by consumers who ultimately cancel — and construct a solved example showing that the consumer-welfare-maximizing friction cap can be interior. Extensions examine friction-induced fatigue and joint regulation of both instruments, characterizing when the complexity response can reverse and showing how joint constraints eliminate endogenous complexity adjustment.
